### Middle Discourses 121
*Majjhima Nikāya 121*

### The Shorter Discourse on Emptiness
*Cūḷasuññatasutta*
> **Pāli:** Evaṁ me sutaṁ—
> **English:** So I have heard.
>
> **Pāli:** ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati pubbārāme migāramātupāsāde.
> **English:** At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in the stilt longhouse of Migāra’s mother in the Eastern Monastery.
>
> **Pāli:** Atha kho āyasmā ānando sāyanhasamayaṁ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṁ etadavoca:
> **English:** Then in the late afternoon, Venerable Ānanda came out of retreat and went to the Buddha. He bowed, sat down to one side, and said to him:
>
> **Pāli:** “Ekamidaṁ, bhante, samayaṁ bhagavā sakkesu viharati nagarakaṁ nāma sakyānaṁ nigamo.
> **English:** “Sir, this one time the Buddha was staying in the land of the Sakyans where they have a town named Townsville.
>
> **Pāli:** Tattha me, bhante, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṁ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṁ:
> **English:** There I heard and learned this in the presence of the Buddha:
>
> **Pāli:** ‘suññatāvihārenāhaṁ, ānanda, etarahi bahulaṁ viharāmī’ti.
> **English:** ‘Ānanda, these days I usually practice the meditation on emptiness.’
>
> **Pāli:** Kacci metaṁ, bhante, sussutaṁ suggahitaṁ sumanasikataṁ sūpadhāritan”ti?
> **English:** I trust I properly heard, learned, applied the mind, and remembered that from the Buddha?”
>
> **Pāli:** “Taggha te etaṁ, ānanda, sussutaṁ suggahitaṁ sumanasikataṁ sūpadhāritaṁ.
> **English:** “Indeed, Ānanda, you properly heard, learned, applied the mind, and remembered that.
>
> **Pāli:** Pubbepāhaṁ, ānanda, etarahipi suññatāvihārena bahulaṁ viharāmi.
> **English:** Now, as before, I usually practice the meditation on emptiness.
>
> **Pāli:** Seyyathāpi, ānanda, ayaṁ migāramātupāsādo suñño hatthigavassavaḷavena, suñño jātarūparajatena, suñño itthipurisasannipātena atthi cevidaṁ asuññataṁ yadidaṁ—
> **English:** Consider this stilt longhouse of Migāra’s mother. It’s empty of elephants, cows, horses, and mares; of gold and silver; and of gatherings of men and women.
>
> **Pāli:** bhikkhusaṅghaṁ paṭicca ekattaṁ;
> **English:** There is only this that is not emptiness, namely, the oneness dependent on the mendicant Saṅgha.
>
> **Pāli:** evameva kho, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā gāmasaññaṁ, amanasikaritvā manussasaññaṁ, araññasaññaṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ.
> **English:** In the same way, a mendicant—ignoring the perception of the village and the perception of people—focuses on the oneness dependent on the perception of wilderness.
>
> **Pāli:** Tassa araññasaññāya cittaṁ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati.
> **English:** Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that perception of wilderness.
>
> **Pāli:** So evaṁ pajānāti:
> **English:** They understand:
>
> **Pāli:** ‘ye assu darathā gāmasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā manussasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṁ darathamattā yadidaṁ—
> **English:** ‘Here there is no stress due to the perception of village or the perception of people.
>
> **Pāli:** araññasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this modicum of stress, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of wilderness.’
>
> **Pāli:** So ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ gāmasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ manussasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṁ asuññataṁ yadidaṁ—
> **English:** They understand: ‘This field of perception is empty of the perception of the village. It is empty of the perception of people.
>
> **Pāli:** araññasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this that is not emptiness, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of wilderness.’
>
> **Pāli:** Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṁ suññaṁ samanupassati, yaṁ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṁ hoti taṁ ‘santamidaṁ atthī’ti pajānāti.
> **English:** And so they regard it as empty of what is not there, but as to what remains they understand that it is present.
>
> **Pāli:** Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.
> **English:** That’s how emptiness manifests in them—genuine, undistorted, and pure.
>
> **Pāli:** Puna caparaṁ, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā manussasaññaṁ, amanasikaritvā araññasaññaṁ, pathavīsaññaṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ.
> **English:** Furthermore, a mendicant—ignoring the perception of people and the perception of wilderness—focuses on the oneness dependent on the perception of earth.
>
> **Pāli:** Tassa pathavīsaññāya cittaṁ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati.
> **English:** Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that perception of earth.
>
> **Pāli:** Seyyathāpi, ānanda, āsabhacammaṁ saṅkusatena suvihataṁ vigatavalikaṁ;
> **English:** As a bull’s hide is rid of folds when fully stretched out by a hundred pegs,
>
> **Pāli:** evameva kho, ānanda, bhikkhu yaṁ imissā pathaviyā ukkūlavikkūlaṁ nadīviduggaṁ khāṇukaṇṭakaṭṭhānaṁ pabbatavisamaṁ taṁ sabbaṁ amanasikaritvā pathavīsaññaṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ.
> **English:** so too, ignoring the hilly terrain, inaccessible riverlands, stumps and thorns, and rugged mountains, they focus on the oneness dependent on the perception of earth.
>
> **Pāli:** Tassa pathavīsaññāya cittaṁ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati.
> **English:** Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that perception of earth.
>
> **Pāli:** So evaṁ pajānāti:
> **English:** They understand:
>
> **Pāli:** ‘ye assu darathā manussasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā araññasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṁ darathamattā yadidaṁ—
> **English:** ‘Here there is no stress due to the perception of people or the perception of wilderness.
>
> **Pāli:** pathavīsaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this modicum of stress, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of earth.’
>
> **Pāli:** So ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ manussasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ araññasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṁ asuññataṁ yadidaṁ—
> **English:** They understand: ‘This field of perception is empty of the perception of people. It is empty of the perception of wilderness.
>
> **Pāli:** pathavīsaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this that is not emptiness, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of earth.’
>
> **Pāli:** Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṁ suññaṁ samanupassati, yaṁ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṁ hoti taṁ ‘santamidaṁ atthī’ti pajānāti.
> **English:** And so they regard it as empty of what is not there, but as to what remains they understand that it is present.
>
> **Pāli:** Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.
> **English:** That’s how emptiness manifests in them—genuine, undistorted, and pure.
>
> **Pāli:** Puna caparaṁ, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā araññasaññaṁ, amanasikaritvā pathavīsaññaṁ, ākāsānañcāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ.
> **English:** Furthermore, a mendicant—ignoring the perception of wilderness and the perception of earth—focuses on the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of infinite space.
>
> **Pāli:** Tassa ākāsānañcāyatanasaññāya cittaṁ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati.
> **English:** Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that perception of the dimension of infinite space.
>
> **Pāli:** So evaṁ pajānāti:
> **English:** They understand:
>
> **Pāli:** ‘ye assu darathā araññasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā pathavīsaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṁ darathamattā yadidaṁ—
> **English:** ‘Here there is no stress due to the perception of wilderness or the perception of earth.
>
> **Pāli:** ākāsānañcāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this modicum of stress, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of infinite space.’
>
> **Pāli:** So ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ araññasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ pathavīsaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṁ asuññataṁ yadidaṁ—
> **English:** They understand: ‘This field of perception is empty of the perception of wilderness. It is empty of the perception of earth.
>
> **Pāli:** ākāsānañcāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this that is not emptiness, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of infinite space.’
>
> **Pāli:** Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṁ suññaṁ samanupassati, yaṁ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṁ hoti taṁ ‘santamidaṁ atthī’ti pajānāti.
> **English:** And so they regard it as empty of what is not there, but as to what remains they understand that it is present.
>
> **Pāli:** Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.
> **English:** That’s how emptiness manifests in them—genuine, undistorted, and pure.
>
> **Pāli:** Puna caparaṁ, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā pathavīsaññaṁ, amanasikaritvā ākāsānañcāyatanasaññaṁ, viññāṇañcāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ.
> **English:** Furthermore, a mendicant—ignoring the perception of earth and the perception of the dimension of infinite space—focuses on the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness.
>
> **Pāli:** Tassa viññāṇañcāyatanasaññāya cittaṁ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati.
> **English:** Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness.
>
> **Pāli:** So evaṁ pajānāti:
> **English:** They understand:
>
> **Pāli:** ‘ye assu darathā pathavīsaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā ākāsānañcāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṁ darathamattā yadidaṁ—
> **English:** ‘Here there is no stress due to the perception of earth or the perception of the dimension of infinite space.
>
> **Pāli:** viññāṇañcāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this modicum of stress, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness.’
>
> **Pāli:** So ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ pathavīsaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ ākāsānañcāyatanasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṁ asuññataṁ yadidaṁ—
> **English:** They understand: ‘This field of perception is empty of the perception of earth. It is empty of the perception of the dimension of infinite space.
>
> **Pāli:** viññāṇañcāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this modicum of stress, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness.’
>
> **Pāli:** Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṁ suññaṁ samanupassati, yaṁ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṁ hoti taṁ ‘santamidaṁ atthī’ti pajānāti.
> **English:** And so they regard it as empty of what is not there, but as to what remains they understand that it is present.
>
> **Pāli:** Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.
> **English:** That’s how emptiness manifests in them—genuine, undistorted, and pure.
>
> **Pāli:** Puna caparaṁ, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā ākāsānañcāyatanasaññaṁ, amanasikaritvā viññāṇañcāyatanasaññaṁ, ākiñcaññāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ.
> **English:** Furthermore, a mendicant—ignoring the perception of the dimension of infinite space and the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness—focuses on the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of nothingness.
>
> **Pāli:** Tassa ākiñcaññāyatanasaññāya cittaṁ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati.
> **English:** Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that perception of the dimension of nothingness.
>
> **Pāli:** So evaṁ pajānāti:
> **English:** They understand:
>
> **Pāli:** ‘ye assu darathā ākāsānañcāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā viññāṇañcāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṁ darathamattā yadidaṁ—
> **English:** ‘Here there is no stress due to the perception of the dimension of infinite space or the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness.
>
> **Pāli:** ākiñcaññāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this modicum of stress, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of nothingness.’
>
> **Pāli:** So ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ ākāsānañcāyatanasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ viññāṇañcāyatanasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṁ asuññataṁ yadidaṁ—
> **English:** They understand: ‘This field of perception is empty of the perception of the dimension of infinite space. It is empty of the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness.
>
> **Pāli:** ākiñcaññāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this that is not emptiness, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of nothingness.’
>
> **Pāli:** Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṁ suññaṁ samanupassati, yaṁ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṁ hoti taṁ ‘santamidaṁ atthī’ti pajānāti.
> **English:** And so they regard it as empty of what is not there, but as to what remains they understand that it is present.
>
> **Pāli:** Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.
> **English:** That’s how emptiness manifests in them—genuine, undistorted, and pure.
>
> **Pāli:** Puna caparaṁ, ānanda bhikkhu amanasikaritvā viññāṇañcāyatanasaññaṁ, amanasikaritvā ākiñcaññāyatanasaññaṁ, nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ.
> **English:** Furthermore, a mendicant—ignoring the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness and the perception of the dimension of nothingness—focuses on the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.
>
> **Pāli:** Tassa nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññāya cittaṁ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati.
> **English:** Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.
>
> **Pāli:** So evaṁ pajānāti:
> **English:** They understand:
>
> **Pāli:** ‘ye assu darathā viññāṇañcāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā ākiñcaññāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṁ darathamattā yadidaṁ—
> **English:** ‘Here there is no stress due to the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness or the perception of the dimension of nothingness.
>
> **Pāli:** nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this modicum of stress, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.’
>
> **Pāli:** So ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ viññāṇañcāyatanasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ ākiñcaññāyatanasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṁ asuññataṁ yadidaṁ—
> **English:** They understand: ‘This field of perception is empty of the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness. It is empty of the perception of the dimension of nothingness.
>
> **Pāli:** nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca ekattan’ti.
> **English:** There is only this that is not emptiness, namely the oneness dependent on the perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.’
>
> **Pāli:** Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṁ suññaṁ samanupassati, yaṁ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṁ hoti taṁ ‘santamidaṁ atthī’ti pajānāti.

>
> **Pāli:** Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.
> **English:** That’s how emptiness manifests in them—genuine, undistorted, and pure.
>
> **Pāli:** Puna caparaṁ, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā ākiñcaññāyatanasaññaṁ, amanasikaritvā nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññaṁ, animittaṁ cetosamādhiṁ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṁ.
> **English:** Furthermore, a mendicant—ignoring the perception of the dimension of nothingness and the perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception—focuses on the oneness dependent on the signless immersion of the heart.
>
> **Pāli:** Tassa animitte cetosamādhimhi cittaṁ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati.
> **English:** Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that signless immersion of the heart.
>
> **Pāli:** So evaṁ pajānāti:
> **English:** They understand:
>
> **Pāli:** ‘ye assu darathā ākiñcaññāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṁ darathamattā yadidaṁ—
> **English:** ‘Here there is no stress due to the perception of the dimension of nothingness or the perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.
>
> **Pāli:** imameva kāyaṁ paṭicca saḷāyatanikaṁ jīvitapaccayā’ti.
> **English:** There is only this modicum of stress, namely that related to the six sense fields dependent on this body and conditioned by life.’
>
> **Pāli:** So ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ ākiñcaññāyatanasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṁ saññāgataṁ nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṁ asuññataṁ yadidaṁ—
> **English:** They understand: ‘This field of perception is empty of the perception of the dimension of nothingness. It is empty of the perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.
>
> **Pāli:** imameva kāyaṁ paṭicca saḷāyatanikaṁ jīvitapaccayā’ti.
> **English:** There is only this that is not emptiness, namely that related to the six sense fields dependent on this body and conditioned by life.’
>
> **Pāli:** Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṁ suññaṁ samanupassati, yaṁ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṁ hoti taṁ ‘santamidaṁ atthī’ti pajānāti.

>

> **English:** Furthermore, a mendicant—ignoring the perception of the dimension of nothingness and the perception of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception—focuses on the oneness dependent on the signless immersion of the heart.
>
> **Pāli:** Tassa animitte cetosamādhimhi cittaṁ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati.
> **English:** Their mind leaps forth, gains confidence, settles down, and becomes decided in that signless immersion of the heart.
>
> **Pāli:** So evaṁ pajānāti:
> **English:** They understand:
>
> **Pāli:** ‘ayampi kho animitto cetosamādhi abhisaṅkhato abhisañcetayito’.
> **English:** ‘Even this signless immersion of the heart is produced by choices and intentions.’
>
> **Pāli:** ‘Yaṁ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṁ abhisañcetayitaṁ tadaniccaṁ nirodhadhamman’ti pajānāti.
> **English:** They understand: ‘But whatever is produced by choices and intentions is impermanent and liable to cessation.’
>
> **Pāli:** Tassa evaṁ jānato evaṁ passato kāmāsavāpi cittaṁ vimuccati, bhavāsavāpi cittaṁ vimuccati, avijjāsavāpi cittaṁ vimuccati.
> **English:** Knowing and seeing like this, their mind is freed from the defilements of sensuality, desire to be reborn, and ignorance.
>
> **Pāli:** Vimuttasmiṁ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṁ hoti.
> **English:** When they’re freed, they know they’re freed.
>
> **Pāli:** ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ, nāparaṁ itthattāyā’ti pajānāti.
> **English:** They understand: ‘Rebirth is ended, the spiritual journey has been completed, what had to be done has been done, there is nothing further for this place.’
>
> **Pāli:** So evaṁ pajānāti:
> **English:** They understand:
>
> **Pāli:** ‘ye assu darathā kāmāsavaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā bhavāsavaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā avijjāsavaṁ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṁ darathamattā yadidaṁ—
> **English:** ‘Here there is no stress due to the defilements of sensuality, desire to be reborn, or ignorance.
