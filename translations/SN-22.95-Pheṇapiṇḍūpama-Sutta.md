### Linked Discourses 22.95
*Saṁyutta Nikāya 22.95*

### 10. Flowers
*10. Pupphavagga*

### A Lump of Foam
*Pheṇapiṇḍūpamasutta*
> **Pāli:** Ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā ayujjhāyaṁ viharati gaṅgāya nadiyā tīre.
> **English:** At one time the Buddha was staying near Ayodhya on the bank of the Ganges river.
>
> **Pāli:** Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi:
> **English:** There the Buddha addressed the mendicants:
>
> **Pāli:** “Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, ayaṁ gaṅgā nadī mahantaṁ pheṇapiṇḍaṁ āvaheyya. Tamenaṁ cakkhumā puriso passeyya nijjhāyeyya yoniso upaparikkheyya. Tassa taṁ passato nijjhāyato yoniso upaparikkhato rittakaññeva khāyeyya, tucchakaññeva khāyeyya, asārakaññeva khāyeyya. Kiñhi siyā, bhikkhave, pheṇapiṇḍe sāro?
> **English:** “Mendicants, suppose this Ganges river was carrying along a big lump of foam. And a person with clear eyes would see it and contemplate it, examining it rationally. And it would appear to them as completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial. For what substance could there be in a lump of foam?
>
> **Pāli:** Evameva kho, bhikkhave, yaṁ kiñci rūpaṁ atītānāgatapaccuppannaṁ …pe… yaṁ dūre santike vā taṁ bhikkhu passati nijjhāyati yoniso upaparikkhati. Tassa taṁ passato nijjhāyato yoniso upaparikkhato rittakaññeva khāyati, tucchakaññeva khāyati, asārakaññeva khāyati. Kiñhi siyā, bhikkhave, rūpe sāro?
> **English:** In the same way, a mendicant sees and contemplates any kind of form at all—past, future, or present; internal or external; coarse or fine; inferior or superior; near or far—examining it rationally. And it appears to them as completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial. For what substance could there be in form?
>
> **Pāli:** Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, saradasamaye thullaphusitake deve vassante udake udakapubbuḷaṁ uppajjati ceva nirujjhati ca. Tamenaṁ cakkhumā puriso passeyya nijjhāyeyya yoniso upaparikkheyya. Tassa taṁ passato nijjhāyato yoniso upaparikkhato rittakaññeva khāyeyya, tucchakaññeva khāyeyya, asārakaññeva khāyeyya. Kiñhi siyā, bhikkhave, udakapubbuḷe sāro?
> **English:** Suppose it was autumn, when the heavens rain heavily, and a bubble on the water forms and pops right away. And a person with clear eyes would see it and contemplate it, examining it rationally. And it would appear to them as completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial. For what substance could there be in a water bubble?
>
> **Pāli:** Evameva kho, bhikkhave, yā kāci vedanā atītānāgatapaccuppannā …pe… yā dūre santike vā taṁ bhikkhu passati nijjhāyati yoniso upaparikkhati. Tassa taṁ passato nijjhāyato yoniso upaparikkhato rittakaññeva khāyati, tucchakaññeva khāyati, asārakaññeva khāyati. Kiñhi siyā, bhikkhave, vedanāya sāro?
> **English:** In the same way, a mendicant sees and contemplates any kind of feeling at all … examining it rationally. And it appears to them as completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial. For what substance could there be in feeling?
>
> **Pāli:** Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, gimhānaṁ pacchime māse ṭhite majjhanhike kāle marīcikā phandati. Tamenaṁ cakkhumā puriso passeyya nijjhāyeyya yoniso upaparikkheyya. Tassa taṁ passato nijjhāyato yoniso upaparikkhato rittakaññeva khāyeyya, tucchakaññeva khāyeyya …pe… kiñhi siyā, bhikkhave, marīcikāya sāro?
> **English:** Suppose that in the last month of summer, at noon, a shimmering mirage appears. And a person with clear eyes would see it and contemplate it, examining it rationally. And it would appear to them as completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial. For what substance could there be in a mirage?
>
> **Pāli:** Evameva kho, bhikkhave, yā kāci saññā …pe….
> **English:** In the same way, a mendicant sees and contemplates any kind of perception at all … examining it carefully. And it appears to them as completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial. For what substance could there be in perception?
>
> **Pāli:** Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, puriso sāratthiko sāragavesī sārapariyesanaṁ caramāno tiṇhaṁ kuṭhāriṁ ādāya vanaṁ paviseyya. So tattha passeyya mahantaṁ kadalikkhandhaṁ ujuṁ navaṁ akukkukajātaṁ. Tamenaṁ mūle chindeyya; mūle chetvā agge chindeyya, agge chetvā pattavaṭṭiṁ vinibbhujeyya. So tassa pattavaṭṭiṁ vinibbhujanto pheggumpi nādhigaccheyya, kuto sāraṁ. Tamenaṁ cakkhumā puriso passeyya nijjhāyeyya yoniso upaparikkheyya. Tassa taṁ passato nijjhāyato yoniso upaparikkhato rittakaññeva khāyeyya, tucchakaññeva khāyeyya, asārakaññeva khāyeyya. Kiñhi siyā, bhikkhave, kadalikkhandhe sāro?
> **English:** Suppose there was a person in need of heartwood. Wandering in search of heartwood, they’d take a sharp axe and enter a forest. There they’d see a big banana trunk, straight and young and grown unskewed. They’d cut it down at the base, cut off the top, and unroll the coiled sheaths. But they wouldn’t even find sapwood, much less heartwood. And a person with clear eyes would see it and contemplate it, examining it rationally. And it would appear to them as completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial. For what substance could there be in a banana trunk?
>
> **Pāli:** Evameva kho, bhikkhave, ye keci saṅkhārā atītānāgatapaccuppannā …pe… ye dūre santike vā taṁ bhikkhu passati nijjhāyati yoniso upaparikkhati. Tassa taṁ passato nijjhāyato yoniso upaparikkhato rittakaññeva khāyati, tucchakaññeva khāyati, asārakaññeva khāyati. Kiñhi siyā, bhikkhave, saṅkhāresu sāro?
> **English:** In the same way, a mendicant sees and contemplates any kind of choices at all … examining them rationally. And they appear to them as completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial. For what substance could there be in choices?
>
> **Pāli:** Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, māyākāro vā māyākārantevāsī vā catumahāpathe māyaṁ vidaṁseyya. Tamenaṁ cakkhumā puriso passeyya nijjhāyeyya yoniso upaparikkheyya. Tassa taṁ passato nijjhāyato yoniso upaparikkhato rittakaññeva khāyeyya, tucchakaññeva khāyeyya, asārakaññeva khāyeyya. Kiñhi siyā, bhikkhave, māyāya sāro?
> **English:** Suppose an illusionist or their apprentice was to demonstrate an illusion at the crossroads. And a person with clear eyes would see it and contemplate it, examining it rationally. And it would appear to them as completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial. For what substance could there be in an illusion?
>
> **Pāli:** Evameva kho, bhikkhave, yaṁ kiñci viññāṇaṁ atītānāgatapaccuppannaṁ …pe… yaṁ dūre santike vā, taṁ bhikkhu passati nijjhāyati yoniso upaparikkhati. Tassa taṁ passato nijjhāyato yoniso upaparikkhato rittakaññeva khāyati, tucchakaññeva khāyati, asārakaññeva khāyati. Kiñhi siyā, bhikkhave, viññāṇe sāro?
> **English:** In the same way, a mendicant sees and contemplates any kind of consciousness at all—past, future, or present; internal or external; coarse or fine; inferior or superior; near or far—examining it rationally. And it appears to them as completely vacuous, hollow, and insubstantial. For what substance could there be in consciousness?
>
> **Pāli:** Evaṁ passaṁ, bhikkhave, sutavā ariyasāvako rūpasmimpi nibbindati, vedanāyapi … saññāyapi … saṅkhāresupi … viññāṇasmimpi nibbindati.
> **English:** Seeing this, a learned noble disciple grows disillusioned with form, feeling, perception, choices, and consciousness.
>
> **Pāli:** Nibbindaṁ virajjati; virāgā vimuccati. Vimuttasmiṁ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṁ hoti …pe…
> **English:** Being disillusioned, desire fades away. When desire fades away they’re freed. When they’re freed, they know they’re freed.
>
> **Pāli:** nāparaṁ itthattāyāti pajānāti”.
> **English:** They understand: ‘… there is nothing further for this place.’”
>
> **Pāli:** Idamavoca bhagavā.
> **English:** That is what the Buddha said.
>
> **Pāli:** Idaṁ vatvāna sugato athāparaṁ etadavoca satthā:
> **English:** Then the Holy One, the Teacher, went on to say:
>
> **Pāli:** “Pheṇapiṇḍūpamaṁ rūpaṁ,
> **English:** “Form is like a lump of foam;
>
> **Pāli:** vedanā bubbuḷūpamā;
> **English:** feeling is like a bubble;
>
> **Pāli:** Marīcikūpamā saññā,
> **English:** perception seems like a mirage;
>
> **Pāli:** saṅkhārā kadalūpamā;
> **English:** choices like a banana plant;
>
> **Pāli:** Māyūpamañca viññāṇaṁ,
> **English:** and consciousness like an illusion:
>
> **Pāli:** desitādiccabandhunā.
> **English:** so taught the kinsman of the Sun.
>
> **Pāli:** Yathā yathā nijjhāyati,
> **English:** However you contemplate them,
>
> **Pāli:** yoniso upaparikkhati;
> **English:** examining them rationally,
>
> **Pāli:** Rittakaṁ tucchakaṁ hoti,
> **English:** they’re vacuous and hollow
>
> **Pāli:** yo naṁ passati yoniso.
> **English:** when you look at them closely.
>
> **Pāli:** Imañca kāyaṁ ārabbha,
> **English:** Concerning this body,
>
> **Pāli:** bhūripaññena desitaṁ;
> **English:** he of vast wisdom has taught
>
> **Pāli:** Pahānaṁ tiṇṇaṁ dhammānaṁ,
> **English:** that when three things are given up,
>
> **Pāli:** rūpaṁ passatha chaḍḍitaṁ.
> **English:** you’ll see this form discarded.
>
> **Pāli:** Āyu usmā ca viññāṇaṁ,
> **English:** When vitality, warmth, and consciousness
>
> **Pāli:** yadā kāyaṁ jahantimaṁ;
> **English:** leave this body,
>
> **Pāli:** Apaviddho tadā seti,
> **English:** it lies dumped there,
>
> **Pāli:** parabhattaṁ acetanaṁ.
> **English:** food for others, insentient.
>
> **Pāli:** Etādisāyaṁ santāno,
> **English:** Such is this continuity,
>
> **Pāli:** māyāyaṁ bālalāpinī;
> **English:** this illusion, this lament of fools.
>
> **Pāli:** Vadhako esa akkhāto,
> **English:** It’s said to be a killer,
>
> **Pāli:** sāro ettha na vijjati.
> **English:** for no core is found here.
>
> **Pāli:** Evaṁ khandhe avekkheyya,
> **English:** An energetic mendicant
>
> **Pāli:** bhikkhu āraddhavīriyo;
> **English:** should examine the aggregates like this,
>
> **Pāli:** Divā vā yadi vā rattiṁ,
> **English:** with situational awareness and mindfulness
>
> **Pāli:** sampajāno paṭissato.
> **English:** whether by day or by night.
>
> **Pāli:** Jaheyya sabbasaṁyogaṁ,
> **English:** They should give up all fetters,
>
> **Pāli:** kareyya saraṇattano;
> **English:** and make a refuge for themselves.
>
> **Pāli:** Careyyādittasīsova,
> **English:** They should live as though their head was on fire,
>
> **Pāli:** patthayaṁ accutaṁ padan”ti.
> **English:** aspiring to the state that does not pass.”
>
> **Pāli:** Tatiyaṁ.
> **English:** 
>
