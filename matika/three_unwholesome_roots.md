# Three Unwholesome Roots (Akusalamūla)

**Navigation**: [[Matika-Index|Pali Canon Vault]] / [[matika/Matika-Index|Mātika]]

Also known as the Three Poisons or Fires that drive the cycle of existence.

## The List

1. **[[greed|Lobha]]**  
   *Greed / Attachment / Lust*
2. **[[hatred|Dosa]]**  
   *Hatred / Anger / Aversion*
3. **[[delusion|Moha]]**  
   *Delusion / Ignorance*

## Related Lists
* Related: [[dependent_origination|Dependent Origination]] (delusion corresponds to the link of ignorance / avijjā; greed maps to craving / taṇhā and clinging / upādāna)
* Related: [[five_hindrances|Five Hindrances]] (greed manifests as sensual desire; hatred manifests as ill-will; delusion underlies doubt and restlessness)
* Related: [[three_marks|Three Marks of Existence]] (unwholesome roots arise from failing to see the impermanent, painful, and non-self nature of reality)

## Canonical References
* [[mn118|MN 118: Ānāpānasati Sutta]] (insight meditation is practiced to eradicate these roots by seeing things as they really are)
* [[dhp_17_kodhavagga|Dhp Ch. 17: Kodhavagga (Anger)]] — v. 254: "rāgañca dosañca pahāya mohaṁ" explicitly lists abandoning greed, hate, and delusion as the mark of the one who has crossed the flood; the chapter traces all moral failures to anger (dosa) as the entry point to the three roots
* [[dhp_24_tanhavagga|Dhp Ch. 24: Taṇhāvagga (Craving)]] — devoted to taṇhā (lobha root) as the fuel of saṃsāra; v. 338-339 compare craving to a tree regrown from an uncut root; v. 356 connects craving, intoxication, and clinging across all three roots
* [[mn19|MN 19: Dvedhāvitakkasutta]] — describes how the Bodhisatta divided his thoughts into two categories: unwholesome thoughts (sensual desire, ill-will, and harmfulness) which stem from the three unwholesome roots, and wholesome thoughts, showing how to abandon the unwholesome and cultivate the wholesome.
* [[snp1_1|Snp 1.1: Uragasutta]] — teaches the shedding of anger, passion, and pride/delusion like a serpent sloughing off its worn-out skin.
* [[dn21|DN 21: Sakkapañhasutta]] — Sakka's inquiry reveals that conflict, jealousy, and stinginess arise from dear/hated things, which arise from desire (*chanda*), which arises from unwholesome thinking rooted in delusion.
